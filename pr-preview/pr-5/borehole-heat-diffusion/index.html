Radial heat diffusion around a borehole

A 1-D forward model of how the ice around a vertical borehole warms while the borehole wall is held at a fixed temperature, and how it relaxes afterwards.

Set the parameters in section 2 and run all cells. Sections 4 to 8 run the model, plot the result, save it to NetCDF, check the numerics, and show how to rerun with different parameters. Sections 9 and 10 look at the temperature in the borehole itself after heating, and at how well the undisturbed ice temperature can be estimated from it by plotting temperature against 1/t.

The notebook needs only numpy, scipy, matplotlib and xarray.

On the project website the longer code cells are collapsed by default. Expand them to see the code.

1. Physical model¶

Assumptions¶

  • Temperature depends only on radial distance rrr from the borehole axis and on time ttt. There is no vertical or azimuthal variation.

  • Heat moves by conduction only: no advection (ice flow, moving water) and no internal heat sources.

  • No latent heat and no phase change. The ice is never heated above 0 °C, so it stays solid. Melting or refreezing at the borehole wall is not modelled.

  • Conductivity kkk, density ρ\rhoρ and specific heat capacity ccc are constants.

  • The borehole interior is not resolved. The domain starts at the borehole wall, r=rbr = r_br=rb​.

Governing equation¶

Take a cylindrical shell of ice of unit height between rrr and r+drr + \mathrm{d}rr+dr. It stores heat ρc T  2πr dr\rho c\,T\;2\pi r\,\mathrm{d}rρcT2πrdr. Fourier’s law gives a radial heat flux −k ∂T/∂r-k\,\partial T/\partial r−k∂T/∂r (W m⁻²), so the heat flowing outward through the cylinder of radius rrr, per metre of borehole, is

Q(r)=−2πr k ∂T∂r.Q(r) = -2\pi r\,k\,\frac{\partial T}{\partial r}.Q(r)=−2πrk∂r∂T​.
(1)

Conservation of energy says the shell warms at the rate heat enters at rrr minus the rate it leaves at r+drr + \mathrm{d}rr+dr:

ρc  2πr dr  ∂T∂t=Q(r)−Q(r+dr)=−∂Q∂r dr.\rho c\;2\pi r\,\mathrm{d}r\;\frac{\partial T}{\partial t} = Q(r) - Q(r + \mathrm{d}r) = -\frac{\partial Q}{\partial r}\,\mathrm{d}r .ρc2πrdr∂t∂T​=Q(r)−Q(r+dr)=−∂r∂Q​dr.
(2)

Substituting QQQ and dividing by 2πr ρc dr2\pi r\,\rho c\,\mathrm{d}r2πrρcdr gives, for constant kkk,

∂T∂t=κ 1r∂∂r ⁣(r ∂T∂r),κ=kρc.\frac{\partial T}{\partial t} = \kappa\,\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\left(r\,\frac{\partial T}{\partial r}\right), \qquad \kappa = \frac{k}{\rho c}.∂t∂T​=κr1​∂r∂​(r∂r∂T​),κ=ρck​.
(3)

Domain, initial and boundary conditions¶

  • Initial condition: T=T0T = T_0T=T0​ everywhere.

  • Borehole wall, r=rbr = r_br=rb​: either a fixed temperature T=ThotT = T_\mathrm{hot}T=Thot​ (Dirichlet) or no heat flux, ∂T/∂r=0\partial T/\partial r = 0∂T/∂r=0 (Neumann). The choice is made separately for each phase.

  • Outer boundary, r=Rmaxr = R_\mathrm{max}r=Rmax​: no heat flux, ∂T/∂r=0\partial T/\partial r = 0∂T/∂r=0. RmaxR_\mathrm{max}Rmax​ stands in for infinity, so it must be far enough away that the warming does not reach it. run_model warns if it does.

Why rbr_brb​ cannot be zero¶

Steady temperature profiles in this geometry have the form T=A+Bln⁡rT = A + B\ln rT=A+Blnr, which diverges as r→0r \to 0r→0. The heat that a cylinder held at ThotT_\mathrm{hot}Thot​ delivers to the ice falls towards zero as the cylinder shrinks, roughly as 1/ln⁡(4κt/rb2)1/\ln(4\kappa t/r_b^2)1/ln(4κt/rb2​), so a line of zero radius held at a fixed temperature transfers no heat at all. A model that put the fixed temperature at r=0r = 0r=0 would have an effective borehole radius set by the size of its first grid cell, and its answer would change whenever the grid changed. The borehole radius is therefore a physical parameter. Because the dependence is logarithmic, the results are only weakly sensitive to it.

Time is measured from the start of heating, so spin-up occupies t<0t < 0t<0 and recovery starts at t=t =t= heating duration.

With a uniform initial temperature and no-flux boundaries the spin-up state is already steady. The phase is kept so that the “before” state is produced by the same solver, and so that the steady-state check (max |dT/dt|, printed by run_model) is in place if the spin-up boundary condition is ever changed.

3. Numerical method¶

Grid¶

The domain [rb,Rmax][r_b, R_\mathrm{max}][rb​,Rmax​] is divided into NNN annular cells, numbered i=0…N−1i = 0 \dots N-1i=0…N−1 outward from the borehole. Cell iii lies between faces ri−1/2r_{i-1/2}ri−1/2​ and ri+1/2r_{i+1/2}ri+1/2​, and the faces are log-spaced:

ri−1/2=rb(Rmaxrb)i/N.r_{i-1/2} = r_b \left(\frac{R_\mathrm{max}}{r_b}\right)^{i/N}.ri−1/2​=rb​(rb​Rmax​​)i/N.
(4)

Every cell is wider than the one before by the same factor, so the grid is fine where gradients are steep (0.9 mm at the wall with the defaults) and coarse far away (0.9 m at RmaxR_\mathrm{max}Rmax​). Cell centres sit at the geometric mean of the two faces, ri=ri−1/2 ri+1/2r_i = \sqrt{r_{i-1/2}\,r_{i+1/2}}ri​=ri−1/2​ri+1/2​​, which is the midpoint in ln⁡r\ln rlnr.

Finite-volume discretisation¶

Integrating the governing equation over cell iii, whose plan-view area is Ai=π (ri+1/22−ri−1/22)A_i = \pi\,(r_{i+1/2}^2 - r_{i-1/2}^2)Ai​=π(ri+1/22​−ri−1/22​), gives

Ai dTidt=κ[ Gi+1/2 (Ti+1−Ti)  −  Gi−1/2 (Ti−Ti−1) ],Gi+1/2=2π ri+1/2ri+1−ri.A_i\,\frac{\mathrm{d}T_i}{\mathrm{d}t} = \kappa\left[\,G_{i+1/2}\,(T_{i+1} - T_i) \;-\; G_{i-1/2}\,(T_i - T_{i-1})\,\right], \qquad G_{i+1/2} = \frac{2\pi\,r_{i+1/2}}{r_{i+1} - r_i}.Ai​dtdTi​​=κ[Gi+1/2​(Ti+1​−Ti​)−Gi−1/2​(Ti​−Ti−1​)],Gi+1/2​=ri+1​−ri​2πri+1/2​​.
(5)

Gi+1/2G_{i+1/2}Gi+1/2​ is the circumference of the face divided by the distance between the two cell centres it separates. The exchange across each face is added to one cell and subtracted from its neighbour, so ∑iAiTi\sum_i A_i T_i∑i​Ai​Ti​ (the heat content divided by ρc\rho cρc) can change only through the two boundary faces. This is what makes the rrr-weighting conservative.

Boundary conditions¶

  • No flux (borehole wall or RmaxR_\mathrm{max}Rmax​): the term for that face is left out.

  • Fixed temperature at the borehole wall: cell 0 exchanges heat with the wall across half a cell, which adds −Gw (T0−Thot)-G_w\,(T_0 - T_\mathrm{hot})−Gw​(T0​−Thot​) to the bracket, with Gw=2πrb/(r0−rb)G_w = 2\pi r_b/(r_0 - r_b)Gw​=2πrb​/(r0​−rb​).

Time stepping¶

The model uses backward Euler. Writing A=diag(Ai)\mathbf{A} = \mathrm{diag}(A_i)A=diag(Ai​) and K\mathbf{K}K for the tridiagonal matrix of the GGG coefficients, each step solves

(A+Δt κ K)T n+1=A T n+Δt κ Gw Thot e0,\left(\mathbf{A} + \Delta t\,\kappa\,\mathbf{K}\right)\mathbf{T}^{\,n+1} = \mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{T}^{\,n} + \Delta t\,\kappa\,G_w\,T_\mathrm{hot}\,\mathbf{e}_0 ,(A+ΔtκK)Tn+1=ATn+ΔtκGw​Thot​e0​,
(6)

where the last term is present only in a fixed-temperature phase. The system is tridiagonal and is solved with scipy.linalg.solve_banded. Because the equation is linear, the code advances the warming T−T0T - T_0T−T0​ instead of TTT, which is equivalent and keeps round-off error from the uniform background out of the small late-time signal.

Each phase starts with a step of dt_initial_s, and each step is dt_growth times longer than the last until it reaches dt_max_days. While it is growing the step is about (dt_growth − 1) × (time since the phase started), so there are the same number of steps in every decade of time. That matches how the solution evolves after a sudden change in boundary condition.

Why backward Euler. Every phase begins with a jump in the boundary condition. Backward Euler damps the sharp gradients this creates and cannot overshoot, so temperatures always stay between T0T_0T0​ and ThotT_\mathrm{hot}Thot​. Crank–Nicolson is more accurate per step but rings after such jumps unless extra damping steps are added. Backward Euler is only first-order accurate in time, but each step is one small tridiagonal solve taking about 20 µs, so the model simply takes many small steps (about 15,000 with the defaults, in well under a second). Prescribed step sizes also make the “halve Δt” convergence test in section 7 exact, which the adaptive steps of scipy.integrate.solve_ivp would not.

Output¶

The solver steps exactly onto every output time, so nothing is interpolated in time. Time series at the monitoring radii are interpolated linearly in ln⁡r\ln rlnr between cell centres, because the grid is uniform in ln⁡r\ln rlnr and conduction profiles are close to linear in it. The temperature on the borehole wall itself is ThotT_\mathrm{hot}Thot​ during a fixed-temperature phase; otherwise it is extrapolated from the first two cells assuming zero gradient at the wall.

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Grid:
    """Log-spaced finite-volume grid in r. All lengths are in metres."""

    r_face: np.ndarray            # (n_cells + 1,) cell faces; r_face[0] = r_b, r_face[-1] = R_max
    r: np.ndarray                 # (n_cells,) cell centres
    cell_area: np.ndarray         # (n_cells,) plan-view area of each annulus (m2)
                                  #            = cell volume per metre of borehole
    face_conductance: np.ndarray  # (n_cells + 1,) G = 2 pi r_face / (distance between cell centres);
                                  #            zero on both boundary faces (no flux)
    wall_conductance: float       # G_w = 2 pi r_b / (r[0] - r_b): links cell 0 to a fixed-temperature wall


def build_grid(r_b, R_max, n_cells):
    """Build the log-spaced grid between the borehole wall r_b and the outer boundary R_max."""
    r_face = np.geomspace(r_b, R_max, n_cells + 1)
    r_face[0], r_face[-1] = r_b, R_max            # guard the end points against round-off
    r = np.sqrt(r_face[:-1] * r_face[1:])         # geometric mean = midpoint in ln(r)
    cell_area = np.pi * (r_face[1:] ** 2 - r_face[:-1] ** 2)
    face_conductance = np.zeros(n_cells + 1)
    face_conductance[1:-1] = 2 * np.pi * r_face[1:-1] / np.diff(r)   # interior faces only
    wall_conductance = 2 * np.pi * r_b / (r[0] - r_b)
    return Grid(r_face, r, cell_area, face_conductance, wall_conductance)


def temperature_tendency(T, grid, kappa, inner_bc, T_wall):
    """dT/dt (K/s) of the finite-volume scheme, written out face by face.

    The implicit solver below uses the same operator in matrix form. This explicit
    version is used for the steady-state check at the end of spin-up.
    """
    # Heat flow through each face in the +r direction, divided by the conductivity k
    flow = np.zeros(T.size + 1)
    flow[1:-1] = -grid.face_conductance[1:-1] * np.diff(T)
    if inner_bc == "dirichlet":
        flow[0] = -grid.wall_conductance * (T[0] - T_wall)
    # flow[0] (unless set above) and flow[-1] stay zero: no-flux boundaries
    return -kappa * np.diff(flow) / grid.cell_area


def conduction_matrix(grid, inner_bc):
    """Tridiagonal matrix K in the banded storage used by scipy.linalg.solve_banded.

    The scheme is  A dT/dt = -kappa K T + (boundary term),  with A = diag(cell_area).
    Row 0 of the result holds the super-diagonal, row 1 the diagonal, row 2 the sub-diagonal.
    """
    to_inner = grid.face_conductance[:-1].copy()   # link from each cell to its inner neighbour
    to_outer = grid.face_conductance[1:]           # link to its outer neighbour (zero at R_max)
    if inner_bc == "dirichlet":
        to_inner[0] = grid.wall_conductance        # cell 0 exchanges heat with the wall
    K = np.zeros((3, grid.r.size))
    K[0, 1:] = -to_outer[:-1]
    K[1, :] = to_inner + to_outer
    K[2, :-1] = -to_inner[1:]
    return K

def phase_output_times(duration, extra_times, p):
    """Times (s after the start of a phase) at which the solution is stored.

    Log-spaced after the change in boundary condition, never more than
    output_max_gap_days apart, plus any extra times and the end of the phase.
    """
    parts = [np.array([duration])]
    if duration > p["output_first_s"]:
        parts.append(np.geomspace(p["output_first_s"], duration, p["n_output_log"])[:-1])
    max_gap = p["output_max_gap_days"] * SECONDS_PER_DAY
    parts.append(np.arange(max_gap, duration, max_gap))
    extra_times = np.asarray(extra_times, dtype=float)
    parts.append(extra_times[(extra_times > 0) & (extra_times < duration)])
    return np.unique(np.concatenate(parts))


def phase_step_times(duration, output_times, p):
    """Times (s after the start of a phase) at the end of every time step.

    Step lengths grow geometrically from dt_initial_s up to dt_max_days. The output
    times are merged in, which splits a step in two wherever one falls inside it,
    so the solver lands exactly on every output time.
    """
    dt_max = p["dt_max_days"] * SECONDS_PER_DAY
    scheduled = []
    t, dt = 0.0, p["dt_initial_s"]
    while t + dt < duration:
        t += dt
        scheduled.append(t)
        dt = min(dt * p["dt_growth"], dt_max)
        if len(scheduled) > 2_000_000:
            raise ValueError("More than 2 million time steps in one phase: increase dt_growth or dt_max_days.")
    return np.unique(np.concatenate([scheduled, output_times]))   # output_times ends at `duration`


def advance_phase(T, grid, kappa, inner_bc, T_wall, step_times, output_times):
    """Advance T through one phase with backward Euler.

    Returns the final temperature and an array (n_output, n_cells) of the stored profiles.
    """
    K = conduction_matrix(grid, inner_bc)
    store = np.isin(step_times, output_times)
    T_stored = np.empty((output_times.size, T.size))
    n_stored = 0
    t_previous = 0.0
    for t_new, store_this_step in zip(step_times, store):
        dt = t_new - t_previous
        # Solve (A + dt kappa K) T_new = A T_old + dt kappa G_w T_wall e_0
        lhs = (dt * kappa) * K
        lhs[1] += grid.cell_area
        rhs = grid.cell_area * T
        if inner_bc == "dirichlet":
            rhs[0] += dt * kappa * grid.wall_conductance * T_wall
        T = solve_banded((1, 1), lhs, rhs, overwrite_ab=True, overwrite_b=True, check_finite=False)
        t_previous = t_new
        if store_this_step:
            T_stored[n_stored] = T
            n_stored += 1
    return T, T_stored

def zero_gradient_face_value(T_near, T_next, d_near, d_next):
    """Temperature on a boundary face with dT/dr = 0 there.

    Fits T = T_face + a d^2 (d = distance from the face) through the two cells nearest the face.
    """
    return T_near - (T_next - T_near) * d_near**2 / (d_next**2 - d_near**2)


def temperature_at_radii(ds, radii):
    """Interpolate stored temperature profiles to any radii (m) between r_b and R_max.

    Interpolation is linear in ln(r). The wall and outer-boundary temperatures are
    included, so r_b and R_max themselves are valid. Returns a DataArray (time, r_monitor).
    """
    radii = np.atleast_1d(np.asarray(radii, dtype=float))
    r_b, R_max = float(ds.attrs["r_b"]), float(ds.attrs["R_max"])
    if radii.min() < r_b or radii.max() > R_max:
        raise ValueError(f"Radii must lie between r_b = {r_b:g} m and R_max = {R_max:g} m.")
    r_ext = np.concatenate([[r_b], ds["r"].values, [R_max]])
    T_ext = np.column_stack([ds["T_wall"].values, ds["T"].values, ds["T_outer"].values])
    x, x_ext = np.log(radii), np.log(r_ext)
    j = np.clip(np.searchsorted(x_ext, x, side="right") - 1, 0, x_ext.size - 2)   # interval containing each radius
    weight = (x - x_ext[j]) / (x_ext[j + 1] - x_ext[j])
    values = (1 - weight) * T_ext[:, j] + weight * T_ext[:, j + 1]
    return xr.DataArray(
        values,
        dims=("time", "r_monitor"),
        coords={
            "time": ds["time"],
            "r_monitor": ("r_monitor", radii, {"units": "m", "long_name": "monitoring radius"}),
        },
        attrs={"units": "degC", "long_name": "ice temperature at the monitoring radii"},
    )


def check_params(p):
    """Stop early on parameter values the model cannot handle."""
    if not 0 < p["r_b"] < p["R_max"]:
        raise ValueError("Need 0 < r_b < R_max. The borehole radius cannot be zero (see section 1).")
    if p["n_cells"] < 10:
        raise ValueError("n_cells must be at least 10.")
    if p["dt_initial_s"] <= 0 or p["dt_growth"] < 1 or p["dt_max_days"] <= 0:
        raise ValueError("Need dt_initial_s > 0, dt_growth >= 1 and dt_max_days > 0.")
    for name in PHASES:
        if p[f"inner_bc_{name}"] not in ("dirichlet", "neumann"):
            raise ValueError(f"inner_bc_{name} must be 'dirichlet' or 'neumann'.")
        if p[f"{name}_days"] < 0:
            raise ValueError(f"{name}_days cannot be negative.")
    radii = np.asarray(p["monitor_radii_m"], dtype=float)
    if radii.size and (radii.min() < p["r_b"] or radii.max() > p["R_max"]):
        raise ValueError("monitor_radii_m must lie between r_b and R_max.")
    if p["T_hot"] > 0:
        warnings.warn("T_hot is above 0 °C. Ice would melt, and this model has no phase change.", stacklevel=3)


def as_netcdf_attribute(value):
    """Convert a parameter value into something NetCDF can store as a global attribute."""
    if value is None:
        return "none"
    if isinstance(value, (bool, np.bool_)):
        return int(value)
    if isinstance(value, (str, int, float, np.number)):
        return value
    return np.asarray(value, dtype=float)   # lists of numbers


def run_model(params, verbose=True):
    """Run spin-up, heating and recovery and return the stored output as an xarray.Dataset.

    `params` is a dictionary like the one in section 2. To change a parameter without
    editing that cell, pass a modified copy:  run_model({**params, "heating_days": 3}).
    """
    p = dict(params)
    check_params(p)
    T0, T_hot = float(p["T0"]), float(p["T_hot"])
    kappa = p["k"] / (p["rho"] * p["c"])   # thermal diffusivity (m2 s-1)
    grid = build_grid(p["r_b"], p["R_max"], p["n_cells"])
    duration = {name: p[f"{name}_days"] * SECONDS_PER_DAY for name in PHASES}
    inner_bc = {name: p[f"inner_bc_{name}"] for name in PHASES}
    extra_output = {
        "spinup": [],
        "heating": np.asarray(p["profile_times_heating_days"], dtype=float) * SECONDS_PER_DAY,
        "recovery": np.asarray(p["profile_times_recovery_days"], dtype=float) * SECONDS_PER_DAY,
    }

    # ---- Time integration, one phase after another ----------------------------
    # The equation is linear, so the solver advances the warming, T - T0, instead of T.
    # The two are equivalent, and this keeps round-off error from the uniform background
    # out of the small late-time signal.
    wall_clock_start = time.perf_counter()
    warming = np.zeros(p["n_cells"])          # initial condition: T = T0 everywhere
    wall_warming = T_hot - T0                 # value held at the wall in a "dirichlet" phase
    t_phase_start = -duration["spinup"]       # t = 0 is the start of heating
    out_time = [np.array([t_phase_start])]    # the initial state is the first stored output
    out_warming = [warming[np.newaxis, :].copy()]
    out_phase = [np.array(["initial"])]
    out_bc = [np.array(["none"])]
    n_steps = 0
    spinup_max_tendency = np.nan
    for name in PHASES:
        if duration[name] > 0:
            output_times = phase_output_times(duration[name], extra_output[name], p)
            step_times = phase_step_times(duration[name], output_times, p)
            warming, warming_stored = advance_phase(warming, grid, kappa, inner_bc[name], wall_warming,
                                                    step_times, output_times)
            out_time.append(t_phase_start + output_times)
            out_warming.append(warming_stored)
            out_phase.append(np.full(output_times.size, name))
            out_bc.append(np.full(output_times.size, inner_bc[name]))
            n_steps += step_times.size
        if name == "spinup":
            # Steady-state check: how fast is T still changing when heating is about to start?
            spinup_max_tendency = np.abs(
                temperature_tendency(warming, grid, kappa, inner_bc[name], wall_warming)).max()
        t_phase_start += duration[name]
    elapsed = time.perf_counter() - wall_clock_start

    time_days = np.concatenate(out_time) / SECONDS_PER_DAY
    warming_all = np.concatenate(out_warming)   # (n_time, n_cells)
    phase = np.concatenate(out_phase)
    bc = np.concatenate(out_bc)

    # ---- Warming on the two boundary faces --------------------------------------
    r, r_face = grid.r, grid.r_face
    warming_wall = zero_gradient_face_value(warming_all[:, 0], warming_all[:, 1],
                                            r[0] - r_face[0], r[1] - r_face[0])
    warming_wall = np.where(bc == "dirichlet", wall_warming, warming_wall)   # held at T_hot in a dirichlet phase
    warming_outer = zero_gradient_face_value(warming_all[:, -1], warming_all[:, -2],
                                             r_face[-1] - r[-1], r_face[-1] - r[-2])

    # ---- Heat content relative to the uniform background, per metre of borehole
    heat_content = p["rho"] * p["c"] * warming_all @ grid.cell_area

    # ---- Outer-boundary checks --------------------------------------------------
    t_end = duration["heating"] + duration["recovery"]
    diffusion_length = np.sqrt(kappa * t_end)
    outer_perturbation = np.abs(warming_outer).max()
    if diffusion_length > p["R_max"] / 4:
        warnings.warn(
            f"R_max = {p['R_max']:g} m is only {p['R_max'] / diffusion_length:.1f} diffusion lengths "
            f"(sqrt(kappa t_end) = {diffusion_length:.1f} m). The outer boundary may influence the "
            "result: make R_max at least 4 diffusion lengths.",
            stacklevel=2,
        )
    if outer_perturbation > p["outer_tolerance_K"]:
        warnings.warn(
            f"The temperature at R_max changed by up to {outer_perturbation:.2e} K, more than the "
            f"tolerance of {p['outer_tolerance_K']:.0e} K. Increase R_max.",
            stacklevel=2,
        )

    # ---- Package the output -----------------------------------------------------
    attrs = {
        "title": "1-D radial heat diffusion around a borehole",
        "time_origin": "time = 0 at the start of the heating phase",
    }
    attrs.update({key: as_netcdf_attribute(value) for key, value in p.items()})
    attrs.update(
        kappa=kappa,                                           # m2 s-1
        n_time_steps=n_steps,
        spinup_max_abs_dTdt_K_per_s=spinup_max_tendency,
        diffusion_length_m=diffusion_length,
        outer_boundary_max_perturbation_K=outer_perturbation,
    )
    ds = xr.Dataset(
        data_vars={
            "T": (("time", "r"), T0 + warming_all, {"units": "degC", "long_name": "ice temperature"}),
            "T_wall": ("time", T0 + warming_wall, {
                "units": "degC", "long_name": "temperature at the borehole wall, r = r_b"}),
            "T_outer": ("time", T0 + warming_outer, {
                "units": "degC", "long_name": "temperature at the outer boundary, r = R_max"}),
            "heat_content": ("time", heat_content, {
                "units": "J m-1",
                "long_name": "heat content of the ice relative to uniform T0, per metre of borehole",
            }),
            "phase": ("time", phase, {
                "long_name": "phase of the forcing schedule that produced this output "
                             "(the phase whose boundary condition was active up to this time)",
            }),
            "inner_bc": ("time", bc, {
                "long_name": "borehole-wall boundary condition active up to this time",
                "comment": "dirichlet: T(r_b) = T_hot; neumann: dT/dr = 0; none: initial state",
            }),
            "cell_area": ("r", grid.cell_area, {
                "units": "m2",
                "long_name": "plan-view area of each annular grid cell (volume per metre of borehole)",
            }),
        },
        coords={
            "time": ("time", time_days, {"units": "day", "long_name": "time since the start of heating"}),
            "r": ("r", grid.r, {"units": "m", "long_name": "radial distance from the borehole axis (cell centres)"}),
        },
        attrs=attrs,
    )
    monitor_radii = np.unique(np.concatenate([[p["r_b"]], np.asarray(p["monitor_radii_m"], dtype=float)]))
    ds["T_monitor"] = temperature_at_radii(ds, monitor_radii)

    if verbose:
        dr = np.diff(grid.r_face)
        seconds_per_year = 365 * SECONDS_PER_DAY
        print(f"Grid: {p['n_cells']} log-spaced cells, {dr[0] * 1e3:.2f} mm wide at the borehole wall "
              f"and {dr[-1]:.2f} m wide at R_max")
        print(f"Thermal diffusivity: kappa = {kappa:.3e} m2/s ({kappa * seconds_per_year:.1f} m2/yr)")
        print(f"Steady-state check at the end of spin-up: max |dT/dt| = {spinup_max_tendency:.1e} K/s "
              f"({spinup_max_tendency * seconds_per_year:.1e} K/yr)")
        print(f"Outer boundary: sqrt(kappa t_end) = {diffusion_length:.1f} m, so R_max is "
              f"{p['R_max'] / diffusion_length:.1f} diffusion lengths away; "
              f"max |T(R_max) - T0| = {outer_perturbation:.1e} K (tolerance {p['outer_tolerance_K']:.0e} K)")
        print(f"Took {n_steps:,} time steps in {elapsed:.2f} s and stored {time_days.size} output times")
    return ds

def format_duration(days):
    """Short label for a duration given in days, e.g. '1 h', '30 d', '3 yr'."""
    years = days / 365
    if days >= 365 and abs(years - round(years)) < 1e-6:
        return f"{round(years)} yr"
    if days >= 1:
        return f"{days:.4g} d"
    if days >= 1 / 24:
        return f"{days * 24:.3g} h"
    return f"{days * 1440:.3g} min"


def sample_ramp(ramp, n):

def refine(params, dx_factor=1, dt_factor=1):
    """Copy of params with the grid spacing divided by dx_factor and every time step divided by dt_factor."""
    refined = dict(params)
    refined["n_cells"] = params["n_cells"] * dx_factor
    refined["dt_initial_s"] = params["dt_initial_s"] / dt_factor
    refined["dt_growth"] = 1 + (params["dt_growth"] - 1) / dt_factor
    refined["dt_max_days"] = params["dt_max_days"] / dt_factor
    return refined

(c) Heating phase against the exact solution¶

For an infinite medium initially at T0T_0T0​ outside a cylinder whose surface is held at ThotT_\mathrm{hot}Thot​ from t=0t = 0t=0, the Laplace transform of the solution is (Carslaw & Jaeger, Conduction of Heat in Solids, 2nd ed., §13.5)

(7)

where K0K_0K0​ is a modified Bessel function. Integrating the wall heat flow in time gives the transform of the total heat delivered per metre of borehole, 2πrbk (Thot−T0) q K1(qrb) / (s2K0(qrb))2\pi r_b k\,(T_\mathrm{hot} - T_0)\,q\,K_1(q r_b)\,/\,(s^2 K_0(q r_b))2πrb​k(Thot​−T0​)qK1​(qrb​)/(s2K0​(qrb​)). Both are inverted numerically here with the fixed-Talbot method (Abate & Valkó, 2004, Int. J. Numer. Meth. Engng 60, 979–993). The comparison applies to the heating phase only, and only when it is a fixed-temperature phase that starts from a uniform temperature.

def invert_laplace(F, t, n_terms=32):
    """Numerical inverse Laplace transform f(t) by the fixed-Talbot method.

    F must accept a complex array s of shape (m, 1) and return an array of shape (m, n),
    so that n values (for example n radii) are inverted together.
    """
    theta = np.arange(1, n_terms) * np.pi / n_terms
    shift = 2 * n_terms / (5 * t)
    cot = 1 / np.tan(theta)
    s = shift * theta * (cot + 1j)                 # points on the Talbot contour
    sigma = theta + (theta * cot - 1) * cot
    on_contour = (np.exp(t * s) * (1 + 1j * sigma))[:, np.newaxis] * F(s[:, np.newaxis])
    on_axis = 0.5 * np.exp(shift * t) * F(np.array([[shift + 0j]]))[0]
    return (shift / n_terms) * (on_axis + on_contour.sum(axis=0)).real


def exact_heating_temperature(r, t, p):
    """Exact T(r, t) (°C) during fixed-temperature heating of an infinite medium; t in seconds."""
    kappa = p["k"] / (p["rho"] * p["c"])
    r = np.atleast_1d(np.asarray(r, dtype=float))

    def F(s):
        q = np.sqrt(s / kappa)
        # K0(q r) / K0(q r_b), written with exponentially scaled Bessel functions to avoid underflow
        return kve(0, q * r) / kve(0, q * p["r_b"]) * np.exp(-q * (r - p["r_b"])) / s

    return p["T0"] + (p["T_hot"] - p["T0"]) * invert_laplace(F, t)


def exact_heat_delivered(t, p):
    """Exact heat (J per metre of borehole) delivered to the ice after t seconds of heating."""
    kappa = p["k"] / (p["rho"] * p["c"])

    def F(s):
        q = np.sqrt(s / kappa)
        return 2 * np.pi * p["r_b"] * p["k"] * (p["T_hot"] - p["T0"]) * q * kve(1, q * p["r_b"]) / kve(0, q * p["r_b"]) / s**2

    return invert_laplace(F, t)[0]

9. Borehole temperature after heating¶

In the field only the temperature in the borehole can be measured, some time after the hole has been at the melting point. This section looks at the model’s borehole temperature during recovery, which is the temperature at the borehole wall (T_wall). Section 10 then tests how well the undisturbed ice temperature T0T_0T0​ can be recovered from it.

10. Estimating the undisturbed temperature from 1/t¶

Long after heating, the heat QQQ left in the ice per metre of borehole behaves as if it had been released all at once along a line, and the borehole temperature approaches

T(t)=T0+Q4πk t.T(t) = T_0 + \frac{Q}{4\pi k\,t}.T(t)=T0​+4πktQ​.
(8)

This is the relation Humphrey and Echelmeyer (1990, J. Glaciol. 36, 287–298) give for sensors frozen into hot-water-drilled holes, as quoted by McDowell et al. (2021, The Cryosphere 15, 897–907, supplement eq. S2). Plotted against 1/t1/t1/t the temperature is a straight line, and its intercept at 1/t=01/t = 01/t=0 is T0T_0T0​. Neither QQQ nor kkk needs to be known.

The straight line is only reached when ttt is much longer than the heating duration sss, because the heat was delivered over a finite time and not all at once. For a source of constant power lasting a time sss, the corresponding result is T=T0+Q4πk sln⁡(1+s/t)T = T_0 + \dfrac{Q}{4\pi k\,s}\ln(1 + s/t)T=T0​+4πksQ​ln(1+s/t) (Lachenbruch and Brewer, 1959, U.S. Geological Survey Bulletin 1083-C), which bends away from the 1/t1/t1/t line when ttt is comparable to sss.

Three ways of estimating T0T_0T0​ are compared here, with ttt measured from the end of heating:

  • 1/t fit: a least-squares straight line through TTT against 1/t1/t1/t. The intercept is the estimate.

  • ln(1 + s/t) fit: the same, with ln⁡(1+s/t)\ln(1 + s/t)ln(1+s/t) on the horizontal axis. This needs the heating duration sss.

  • Last reading: no extrapolation. The latest reading is taken as the estimate.

The readings are the model’s borehole temperature on the days of each schedule, with no measurement noise, so the errors below are the errors of the methods themselves.

# zero as t tends to infinity. T is fitted against one of them and extrapolated to zero.
TIME_FUNCTIONS = {
    "1/t fit": lambda t, s: 1 / t,
    "ln(1 + s/t) fit": lambda t, s: np.log(1 + s / t),
}


def estimate_T0(days, T, heating_days, method="1/t fit"):
    """Estimate the undisturbed temperature from borehole temperatures T (°C) read on `days` after heating.

    Returns (intercept, slope) of the least-squares straight line. The intercept is the estimate.
    """
    x = TIME_FUNCTIONS[method](np.asarray(days, dtype=float), heating_days)
    slope, intercept = np.polyfit(x, T, 1)
    return intercept, slope


def estimation_errors(ds, schedules):
    """Error (K) of each estimate of T0 for each schedule. Positive means the estimate is too warm."""
    T0, heating_days = float(ds.attrs["T0"]), float(ds.attrs["heating_days"])
    errors = {}
    for name, days in schedules.items():
        T = borehole_temperature(ds, days)
        errors[name] = {"last reading": T[-1] - T0}
        for method in TIME_FUNCTIONS:
            errors[name][method] = estimate_T0(days, T, heating_days, method)[0] - T0
    return errors


def noise_amplification(days, heating_days=None, method="1/t fit"):
    """Standard deviation of the fitted intercept divided by that of one reading, for independent reading errors.

    heating_days is only needed for the ln(1 + s/t) fit.
    """
    x = TIME_FUNCTIONS[method](np.asarray(days, dtype=float), heating_days)
    return np.sqrt(np.sum(x**2) / (x.size * np.sum((x - x.mean()) ** 2)))

How long to wait¶

The schedules above are examples. A more general view is to ask what a fit gives when all the readings are taken close to a time ttt after heating. A straight line through those readings is the tangent to the curve of TTT against the time function x(t)x(t)x(t) of the fit, and its intercept is in error by (T−T0)−x dT/dx(T - T_0) - x\,\mathrm{d}T/\mathrm{d}x(T−T0​)−xdT/dx. In each figure the left panel compares that error (dashed) with the error of taking a reading at face value (solid). The right panel shows the ratio of the two against time measured in heating durations.

The first figure is for the 1/t fit, where the curves for different heating durations nearly coincide. The second is for the ln(1 + s/t) fit, on the same axes. Its curves are lower, and they do not coincide: that fit assumes heat was delivered at a constant rate, but with the wall held at a fixed temperature the rate falls as heating goes on, and proportionally more over a short heating period.

TIME_FUNCTION_LOG_SLOPES = {
    "1/t fit": lambda t, s: -np.ones_like(t),
    "ln(1 + s/t) fit": lambda t, s: -(s / (t + s)) / np.log(1 + s / t),
}


def fraction_left_by_fit(ds, method="1/t fit", points_per_decade=24):
    """Error of a fit to readings taken close to time t, as a fraction of the disturbance at time t.

    Returns (t in days since heating ended, disturbance in K, fraction). The fraction is
    1 - [d ln(T - T0) / d ln(t)] / [d ln(x) / d ln(t)]. The slope of the disturbance comes
    from a cubic spline through the stored output on log-log axes.
    """
    t, T = borehole_recovery(ds)
    heating_days = float(ds.attrs["heating_days"])
    log_t_stored, log_disturbance_stored = np.log(t), np.log(T - float(ds.attrs["T0"]))
    # Thin the stored output so that neighbouring spline knots are not almost on top of each other
    knots = [0]
    for index in range(1, t.size):
        if log_t_stored[index] - log_t_stored[knots[-1]] >= 0.06:
            knots.append(index)
    spline = CubicSpline(log_t_stored[knots], log_disturbance_stored[knots])
    n_points = int(points_per_decade * np.log10(t[knots[-1]] / t[0])) + 1
    log_t = np.linspace(log_t_stored[0], log_t_stored[knots[-1]], n_points)
    t = np.exp(log_t)
    fraction = 1 - spline(log_t, 1) / TIME_FUNCTION_LOG_SLOPES[method](t, heating_days)
    return t, np.exp(spline(log_t)), fraction

The best estimate from readings within one year¶

Suppose a logger records the borehole temperature once a day, and every reading comes from the first year after heating. With that many readings, what is left to choose is which fit to use and which readings to put into it. Two things pull in opposite directions:

  • Starting the fit later reduces the systematic error of the method, because later readings are closer to the long-time decay.

  • Starting later leaves fewer readings, spread over a shorter stretch of the fitting axis, so noise in the readings is amplified more.

Three fits are compared, each using the daily readings from a chosen first day up to day 365. Two are the straight-line fits used above. The third is T=T0+A/(t+t0)T = T_0 + A/(t + t_0)T=T0​+A/(t+t0​), where the time shift t0t_0t0​ is fitted along with T0T_0T0​ and AAA. The shift allows for the heat having been delivered over a period before t=0t = 0t=0, so this fit does not need the heating duration.

The top row of the figure is the systematic error with noise-free readings; values below 0.01 mK are beyond the numerical accuracy of the model and are drawn at 0.01 mK. The bottom row is the noise factor: the standard deviation of the estimate divided by the standard deviation of a single reading. The table then combines the two for the reading noise levels in reading_noise_mK, as expected error =systematic2+(noise factor×reading noise)2= \sqrt{\text{systematic}^2 + (\text{noise factor} \times \text{reading noise})^2}=systematic2+(noise factor×reading noise)2​, and reports the first day that minimises it.

def fit_shifted_inverse_time(days, T):
    """Fit T = T0 + A / (t + t0), with the time shift t0 free as well as T0 and A.

    Returns (T0, A, t0, noise factor), where the noise factor is the standard deviation of
    T0 divided by that of one reading, for independent reading errors.
    """
    days = np.asarray(days, dtype=float)
    slope, intercept = np.polyfit(1 / days, T, 1)   # start from the plain 1/t fit
    (T0, A, t0), _ = curve_fit(lambda t, T0, A, t0: T0 + A / (t + t0), days, T, p0=(intercept, slope, 0.0),
                               xtol=1e-12, ftol=1e-12)
    jacobian = np.column_stack([np.ones_like(days), 1 / (days + t0), -A / (days + t0) ** 2])
    return T0, A, t0, np.sqrt(np.linalg.inv(jacobian.T @ jacobian)[0, 0])

  • A 1/t fit to every reading from day 1 is 40 mK too warm, which is worse than the day-365 reading on its own (17 mK).

  • A 1/t fit that starts around day 170 has an expected error of 0.5 mK if the reading noise is 1 mK. With 10 mK reading noise the best start is around day 70 and the expected error is 1.6 mK.

  • The ln(1 + s/t) fit and the fit with a free time shift do better, at about 0.2 mK and 1 mK for the same two noise levels, and they can start much earlier (between about day 7 and day 80). The free time shift does not need the heating duration.

So with readings throughout the first year the estimate can be ten to a hundred times closer than the day-365 reading, and what limits it is noise in the readings, not the method. Reading more often than once a day reduces the noise part in proportion to one over the square root of the number of readings and leaves the systematic part unchanged. A constant sensor offset is not noise in this sense: it shifts every estimate by the same amount.

What this shows¶

These numbers are for the default parameters (background −5 °C, wall held at 0 °C, borehole radius 5 cm).

  • Readings from the first three weeks are far from the undisturbed temperature. Three weeks after 7 days of heating the borehole is still 0.25 K too warm. After 1 day of heating it is 0.06 K too warm.

  • A 1/t fit that includes readings from the first few days does not help. After 7 days of heating, a fit to days 1–21 gives an estimate 0.29 K too warm, which is slightly worse than the last reading on its own. After 1 day of heating the same fit is 0.04 K too warm against 0.06 K for the last reading. The early readings are taken before the temperature has reached the 1/t line, and they carry the most weight in the fit because they lie furthest along the 1/t axis.

  • Leaving out the early readings helps a lot. A fit to days 8–21 only is 0.08 K too warm after 7 days of heating and 0.005 K too warm after 1 day.

  • Rule of thumb: a 1/t fit to readings taken around time ttt leaves a fraction of about s/(s+2t)s/(s + 2t)s/(s+2t) of the disturbance present at that time, where sss is the heating duration. To do ten times better than the raw reading, the readings have to be taken five to six heating durations after the end of heating.

  • Fitting against ln(1 + s/t) gives errors five to ten times smaller than the 1/t fit to the same readings (0.03 K for days 1–21 after 7 days of heating), but it needs the heating duration.

  • After a year the reading itself is within 0.02 K after 7 days of heating and within 0.004 K after 1 day. A 1/t line through readings at one and two years removes what is left, to within the numerical accuracy of the model, but it multiplies measurement noise by 2.2.

  • Early and late readings should not go into one unweighted fit. Adding a one-year reading to the fit of days 1–21 still gives an estimate 0.27 K too warm after 7 days of heating, far worse than the one-year reading alone (0.017 K), because 21 early readings outweigh one late reading.

  • With daily readings throughout the first year, the estimate can be brought to within about 1 mK, provided the fit leaves out the early readings or uses ln(1 + s/t) or a free time shift. The limit is then the noise in the readings.
